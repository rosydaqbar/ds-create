# 2.14 Progress

Progress communicates how much of a task with a known size is complete. It comes in three forms of one family, chosen with `Type`:

- **bar** — a horizontal track that fills from the start; indicates the percent completed for a task or process (uploads, onboarding steps, storage used).
- **circle** — a ring with the number in the middle; a circular completion or resource indicator, often paired with a short label.
- **half-circle** — the same ring as an arch, for dashboards and summary cards where width is generous and height is not.

All three share one construction: a background track and a progress line whose length is driven by `Value`. Use a Spinner (2.15) when the amount of work is unknown.

# 1. Purpose

- Show determinate progress: how much is done and, by implication, how much is left.
- Show a resource level against a known maximum (storage, quota, seats).
- Keep every value built from the same track and line, so changes to colour, thickness or radius propagate to every value.

Total: 165 variants, e.g. `Type=bar, Size=md, Value=40, Placement=right`.

- `Placement` is where the bar's value label sits: `none` (no label), `right` (after the track), `bottom-end` (under the track, aligned to the end), `top` and `bottom` (a floating label that follows the end of the fill).
- `Show label` and `Label` apply to `circle` and `half-circle`: a short text above the number. The bar's value label is always the percentage and has no text property; it is set by `Value`.
- To show a value between the steps, use the nearest variant and override the fill width (bar) or the arc sweep (circle) and the number on the instance; never draw a new shape.

## Construction rules

- **Value-driven.** Every value is the same Track and Fill (bar) or Background and Progress line (circle). `Value` changes only the Fill width or the arc sweep. Never draw eleven unrelated bars or rings.
- **Value 0.** The Fill or Progress line is hidden, not drawn as a zero-length shape. **Value 100** fills the whole Track or ring.
- **Floating labels** are positioned with their centre on the end of the Fill, inside a reserved `Label space`, so the Track sits at the same y in every value. When the centred label would pass the start or end of the track, it aligns to that edge instead.
- **Label placement never moves the track.** `right` shortens the track to make room for the label; all other placements keep the full track width.
- **Circle number and label are optically centred** in the ring: the stack's centre sits on the ring's centre, not its top edge.
- **Half-circle is a shape of the same family**, sharing tokens and stroke model with the circle; it is not rebuilt as a separate component.

## Auto Layout relationship

- Bar: the root stacks track and label by `Placement`; the Track is a fixed-height frame that fills the width, and the Fill is pinned to its start.
- Circle: no Auto Layout on the root; `Ring` and `Number and label` are centred by constraints so the ring can be resized as one.

# 10. Guidelines content

Topics for `2.14 Progress · Guidelines`, in order. Each visual uses real Progress instances.

## Bar or circle

Use a bar where there is width to spare and the progress belongs to a row or a section: uploads, onboarding, a quota line. Use a circle where the indicator is a summary in a card or tile and is paired with a label; use a half-circle in wide, short dashboard cards.

Visual — the file upload row (bar) next to the storage card (circle) from the Overview, captioned with when each fits.

## Determinate vs indeterminate

Progress is for work with a known size. When the size or duration is unknown, use a Spinner (2.15). If the size becomes known part-way, switch from Spinner to Progress.

Visual — three steps of one upload: Spinner "Preparing…", then Progress at 30%, then Progress at 100% with a success message.

## Value-driven construction

Every value is the same track and the same line. To change the look — colour, thickness, radius — edit the tokens or the base layers once, and all 165 variants update. Values between the steps are instance overrides of the same Fill or arc, never new shapes.

Visual — propagation example: (1) `color/fill/brand/solid` highlighted in the token map, (2) the arrow to the Fill layer, (3) bars at 20, 50 and 90 all showing the new colour.

## Label placement

The value label can sit to the right, under the end, or float above or below the fill end. Changing placement never moves the track. Floating labels are useful when the bar is short in height and the exact value matters; static labels are calmer for lists.

Visual — the five placements stacked at `Value=40`, with a dashed guide through the tracks.

## Showing the number

Show the percentage when users make decisions with it (storage, quotas) or when the wait is long. Hide it for quick operations where the movement alone is enough. Round to whole percents; don't show decimals.

Visual — Do/don't pair (`Doc/Do-dont`):
- Don't: "Uploading… 37.462%".
- Do: "Uploading… 37%".

## Progress is not status, and not signal strength

A Progress shows completion toward a maximum. Don't use it as a status badge ("Healthy") or to draw network or signal strength; those are categorical states, not progress, and need their own components.

Visual — Do/don't pairs:
- Don't: a Progress bar at 100% used as a green "Active" marker. Do: a Badge (2.4) "Active".
- Don't: a half-circle used as a signal meter. Do: a dedicated signal indicator from the product's icon set.

## Fake precision and fake progress

Don't move a bar at a steady speed to look busy when you don't know the real progress, and don't stop at 99% for a long time. If you can't measure, use a Spinner.

Visual — Don't: a bar labelled "99%" with "Still working…". Do: a Spinner with "Finishing up…".

## Motion

The Fill or Progress line animates smoothly from the old value to the new one with `motion/duration/base` and `motion/easing/standard`. With reduced motion, it jumps to the new value without animating.

Visual — a filmstrip of the bar moving from 40 to 60, with the reduced-motion version showing only the before and after.

## Content

- Pair a Progress with text that names the task ("Uploading 3 files") and, when helpful, the remaining amount ("2 MB left").
- Use the percent sign with no space ("40%").
- Circle labels are one or two words ("Used", "Goal reached").

## Accessibility

- In code, Progress is a `progressbar` with its minimum, maximum and current value, and an accessible name that says what is progressing.
- Announce completion in text; don't rely on the bar reaching the end.
- The Fill meets the non-text contrast threshold against the Track, and the Track against the surface, in every colour mode.
- Colour is not the only signal: the number or the accompanying text carries the value.

# 11. QA

Fail QA when:
- values are separate shapes with no common Track/Fill or Background/Progress line construction;
- `Value=0` draws a degenerate zero-length arc or a visible dot instead of hiding the line;
- changing `Placement` moves the track or changes its length (other than `right`, which makes room for the label);
- floating labels are not centred on the end of the fill, or leave the component bounds;
- the circle number and label are not optically centred in the ring;
- the half-circle is rebuilt as an unrelated component with its own tokens;
- Progress is shown in the system as a status badge or a signal meter;
- colours, thickness and radius are raw values instead of the tokens in §9.
